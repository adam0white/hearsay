### What clarity does and does not measure

- The adapter exposes a flattened menu of actions for all present people, fixed authored promises, a goal and a next line. The browser first asks the player to select a person. Its conversation offers subjects and traits beyond the adapter subset: the adapter selects Kemal, the latest witnessed incident, reliable-Emre defense, and fixed edge actions. Edge and strategy coverage is limited to that offered subset. The 202–675-word menu findings and equal-promise counts describe this adapter representation; they do not establish that the browser makes that entire menu mandatory reading.
- The adapter supplies no classified `text` field and no disabled-control representation. Goal/next are adapter-supplied copy. No `unmeasured` findings were emitted in this cohort, but that does not mean the full screen was measured. No browser, phone layout (DL-22), visual control check (DL-2), teaching/comprehension check (DL-7), precise acknowledgement latency, or full translation/calendar audit was performed.
- Withdrawing an offered action without an observed reason/event is a review signal; people leaving the garden can change the menu. DL-13 checks matching `answers` over complete 9-second windows. Ambient events alone do not answer an input.
- No language warnings were emitted for exposed load-bearing strings. Names and game prose beyond the adapter fields are not a complete language audit.
- Numeric emotion values, authored promise values, valences, thresholds, edge labels and return decisions are engineering premises. These bounded simulations do not establish engagement for real people, owner acceptance, or a controlled improvement over R2.
